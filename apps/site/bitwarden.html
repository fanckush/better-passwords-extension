Better Passwords

Use Bitwarden with Better Passwords

Your Bitwarden logins in a small menu under the field, strong passwords on sign-up forms, and your verification codes. Cloud or self-hosted, and next to Apple Passwords if you use both.

Add to Chrome Also for Firefox

Free and open source. Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc and Firefox, on Mac, Windows and Linux.

Why not Bitwarden's own extension?

Keep it if you like it. Bitwarden's extension puts your whole vault in the toolbar: cards, identities, notes, Sends and folders, each with its own screens and settings.

Better Passwords only fills and saves logins, from a menu that looks like the one macOS uses for Apple Passwords. Everything else stays in Bitwarden's apps and web vault, where you already manage it.

It's a good fit if you:

  • want autofill that shows up when you need it and stays out of the way otherwise
  • use Apple Passwords too, or are moving from one to the other: logins from both show up in one menu
  • want iCloud's Hide My Email in sign-up forms (needs iCloud+)
  • run your own Bitwarden server

Set it up

  1. Add Better Passwords from the Chrome Web Store or Firefox Add-ons.
  2. In the setup tab that opens, choose Bitwarden.
  3. Pick your server: bitwarden.com, bitwarden.eu, or self-hosted with your server's address.
  4. Sign in with your email and master password. If you use two-step login, enter the code from your authenticator app or email.
  5. Click a login field. Your Bitwarden logins show up in a menu under it.

Bitwarden emails you about a new device the first time you sign in. That's Better Passwords.

After that, it locks when you quit the browser or your computer locks, and you unlock it with your master password from the toolbar. You can change both in the toolbar popup.

Self-hosted servers

Choose self-hosted and type the address you open your web vault at, like https://vault.example.com. A plain http:// address works too, for a server at home or on your own network: the browser asks you to allow that one address.

Vaultwarden isn't tested yet. If you try it, tell us how it went.

Is it safe?

Better Passwords signs in to your Bitwarden server the way Bitwarden's own apps do. Your master password never leaves your computer: only a hash of it is sent, as Bitwarden's apps send it. Your vault is kept encrypted on your computer, like Bitwarden's extension keeps it, and is opened only while unlocked.

It talks only to your Bitwarden server. There's no account with us, no server of ours and no tracking. What's sent is in the privacy policy, how your vault key is kept safe is in the threat model, and the code is public on GitHub.

Questions

Is this made by Bitwarden?

No. Better Passwords is an independent, open-source extension. It signs in to your Bitwarden account the way Bitwarden's own apps do.

Can I keep Bitwarden's extension installed?

Yes, but both would open a menu on the same field. In Bitwarden's extension, go to Settings → Autofill and turn off Show autofill suggestions on form fields.

Do I need Bitwarden Premium?

No. It works with free and paid Bitwarden accounts.

Do logins shared with me show up?

Yes, logins from organizations you're in show up next to your own. New logins are saved to your own vault.

Which two-step login methods work?

Codes from an authenticator app and codes sent by email. Security keys, Duo and single sign-on aren't supported yet.

Does it work on Linux?

Yes. Bitwarden works anywhere Chrome or Firefox runs. Only Apple Passwords needs a Mac or Windows.

Can I use Apple Passwords too?

Yes. Add it from the toolbar popup (Manage…), and logins from both show up in one menu, each with a small tile saying where it's from. More about Apple Passwords.
